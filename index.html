case · focus media

Two brand launch plans for elevator media

At Focus Media, China's largest elevator advertising network, I wrote full launch plans for two small brands: market research, positioning, slogans, creative mock-ups and a media plan.

Instant noodles · my 26 slides of 55

Chencun: claiming an empty shelf

Insight
  • China's instant noodle market reached ¥182.4bn in 2022 and is forecast to hit ¥231.2bn by 2025 (iiMedia).
  • Rice noodles made up 22.3% of instant purchases, but flat rice noodles (he fen) didn't appear in the rankings at all. No brand owned the category.
  • Core buyers: busy, university-educated office workers, mostly women, in tier-1 and tier-2 cities, paying ¥2.6–7.5 a pack.
Positioning

Lead with "Kung Fu He Fen": made in Guangdong, where he fen comes from, and air-dried instead of fried.

Slogans
  • Working late? Pick Chencun He Fen and show your boss some real kung fu.
  • Chencun He Fen: thin enough to see, springy every time.
Media

Elevator TV, posters and smart screens, launching in Guangzhou and Shenzhen, where search interest and the share of young residents were highest.

Baby skincare · my 33 slides of 90

Kingsanhon: a category with no brand

Research
  • Reviewed 4 competitor brands online and visited 5 baby-care stores in Guangzhou, including Aiyingdao, Kidswant and Watsons.
  • Competitors charged ¥50–100 for 50ml and all added other ingredients. Watsons and mall baby sections stocked no camellia oil at all.
Positioning

The baby camellia oil mums can trust: 100% camellia seed oil, nothing added, ¥69 for 30ml.

Plan
  • A six-week elevator campaign in three waves: awareness, then product benefits with a 15-second smart-screen spot (Baby rash? Use Kingsanhon.), then sales.
  • Sales wave: scan a QR code to get a full-size ¥69 bottle for ¥0.09, a gap big enough to signal the product's value.
  • Targeted Guangzhou and Shenzhen first (Guangdong has led China in births for five years) and residential compounds near maternity hospitals.

Slides shown are the parts I wrote. Focus Media's standard media pages and the reference case studies are left out.

case · re-flourishing

Re-Flourishing: bringing a Chinese velvet-flower craft to London

Brief

Ronghua is a traditional craft of flowers made from silk velvet, listed as Chinese intangible cultural heritage. This King's College London course project reinterpreted it for an international audience at Somerset House in December 2024.

What I did
  • As curator and visual director, set the curatorial theme and led the proposal and visitor-flow design.
  • Designed the posters, brochure and key visual, and made a 120-second promotional film.
  • Planned a hands-on workshop where visitors made their own flowers, a rose wall for written responses and a collage of the results.
  • Livestreamed the show on Instagram so people outside London could take part.
